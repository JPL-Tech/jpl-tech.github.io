Who it's for

People who want a 2027 budget in US dollars on one sheet: someone who pays their own bills, a couple who splits household costs, or anyone who would rather write the plan down before the month starts.

The problem

Without a written plan, money goes to categories nobody checks. At the end of the month it is hard to tell what you planned to spend, what you actually spent, and how much was left from your take-home pay. This spreadsheet keeps those three numbers side by side for each of the 12 months of 2027.

What's inside (one Excel file, 6 tabs)

  • Start Here: how to use the file, with an optional note on opening it in Google Sheets.
  • Settings: the budget year (2027) and 15 category names, such as rent, electricity and gas, groceries, dining out, car insurance, health insurance, debt payoff, 401(k), emergency fund and subscriptions. You can rename them.
  • Budget: the blank template with four blocks, each with 12 months: take-home pay (income), Planned (what you expect to spend), Actual (what you really spent), and Difference (actual minus planned, calculated for you). Totals per month and per year.
  • Dashboard: a monthly table (take-home pay, planned, actual, left over, savings, savings rate) and a yearly total. Three charts: income vs planned vs actual by month; where the money went this year (share by category); and what was left over each month.
  • Example Budget and Example Dashboard: the same model filled in with made-up numbers for a two-person household (take-home pay US$ 5,200 a month), so you can see how it works before you type your own.

How to use

  1. Open the file in Excel (.xlsx).
  2. Settings: check the year and rename the categories if you want.
  3. Budget: type your take-home pay for each month (after taxes and payroll deductions).
  4. Budget: type what you plan to spend in each category, for each month.
  5. During the year, type what you really spent in the Actual block.
  6. Dashboard: check the left-over amount, the savings rate and the charts.
